# Pre-fill fact sheet: how wrong it is, what it causes, what is already moving

Compiled 2026-09-01 (evening) for Suph's reply to Brent Denton's 9/1 email "Fwd: Meeting Request from your ASM" (to Curry, Suph, Hartwig). Read-only compilation. Nothing here was sent, posted, or changed.

Framing that the reply needs to hold apart:

- **MODEL** = the engine that turns dwelling inputs into a rebuild cost. Today Verisk 360Value on every quote. Steadily RCE (codename Villa; model = Plinko, served from velma) is built, flag `steadily-replacement-cost-enabled`, rolling out AZ direct then AZ indie first (Rob's 8/31 email, 6 phases). Brent's 9/1 question ("will our own model follow exactly how v360 works or will we be able to make adjustments?") is a model question. What is known: Steadily RCE is our own model with deterministic agent-adjustable modifiers (kitchens, garage type, slope, finish grades, home quality grade is the largest lever: above average +13.7%, custom +37.6%, premium +79.2% vs standard; source: "Villa - BUC-5057 Dwelling Assumption Fields - Disposition (2026-08-17)" gdoc, David Curry). 9/1 kickoff reframed the motivation as "controlling the levers" (book, business-logic.md, verified against transcript).
- **INPUTS** = the pre-filled dwelling fields the model consumes. One pipeline: Smarty address + property lookup seeds a 360Value valuation; the parsed 360Value report writes the dwelling fields; hard defaults when 360Value errors: sqft 2,300, year built 1980 (book, landscape.md "Pre-fill waterfall", code-level 8/24). Steadily RCE never consumes 360Value-sourced values, so in the RCE world the inputs are Smarty + Cape + what humans type (Curry, #eng-sprint-goodbye-360value 8/31; Curry's prefill_nationwide sheet 9/1).
- The SF four-plex (8/18) is the clean proof that inputs, not the engine, drove the gap: same 360Value pricing version, same Above Average grade, different inputs, 29% lower. The Goosehead LA case (9/1) has no input data yet, so it is not yet proven to be an input problem (see Section 5).

Confidence key: **verified** = a Suph/Will/Curry analysis with receipts (Metabase cards, code, spreadsheet) or a code-level book entry; **stated** = someone said it in a thread or meeting, not measured; **stale** = superseded or older than the current read.

## Section 2. What it causes

### 2a. RCE too low (and which direction each input error pushes)

Mechanism (Steadily RCE / Plinko modifiers, from Curry's "Villa - BUC-5057 Dwelling Assumption Fields - Disposition (2026-08-17)" gdoc; 360Value's exact factors are not documented in our files, but the SF case shows the same direction empirically):

- **Kitchens:** 1 = baseline; 2 = +5%; 3 = +10%; 4+ = +15% (skipped when unit count > 1, the multi-family multiplier prices them). Missing kitchens push RCE **DOWN**.
- **Garage:** attached/built-in = full enclosed-garage rate; basement drive-under -3.46%; carport -2.38% to -5.95% depending on walls. A missing garage pushes RCE **DOWN**.
- **Basement:** finished_basement_percent 0 to +3.79% on top of the basement itself; "no effect on a home with no basement". Smarty basement_sqft passes straight to the model (Rob 9/1). A basement the pre-fill does not know about pushes RCE **DOWN** (Datha's case).
- **Bedrooms / bathrooms:** consumed by the model (Smarty-enriched inputs; user edits not yet ingested, see Section 4). Missing rooms push RCE **DOWN**.
- **Home quality grade:** economy -10.2%, above_average +13.7%, custom +37.6%, premium +79.2% vs standard; "the largest lever we have"; falls through to ZIP-based inference only when missing. Kouvaris's SF complaint was partly about this ("Above Average" not matching Bay Area reality), but Suph found the grade was the SAME across all three carrier runs, so the gap was inputs.
- **Finishes:** drywall to plaster +7%; slope none to steep +12%; carpet to hardwood +0.5%, tile +1.0%, vinyl -3%; interior finish standard to luxury +5%. Pre-fill defaults here (carpet 90%, 1 heating system in the SF case) push RCE **DOWN** when the truth is nicer.
- **Square footage: the direction is the OTHER way.** UW shrinks sqft on 58% of its sqft fixes (median 357 sq ft / 17.5%). On those quotes our pre-fill was making RCE too HIGH, not too low. Bake-off artifact 8/17: "accurate does not equal bigger."
- **Year built:** feed skews too new (71% of UW fixes make the home older). Effect on RCE not documented in our files; effect on premium is up.

### 2b. Underwriting time and alerts

- Dwelling age (DAMR) is the highest-volume alert: ~5,044 UW touches/mo, ~2,200 forced reviews/mo; 81.5% of asked referrals approved; 46.5% approved touching nothing; 25.3% add the roof exclusion; **28.2% fix our own data**; 8.4% rejected (Do What the Underwriter Does 8/24; BUC-4962 census).
- The manual waterfall UWs run to fix it: **LandGlide first, then Zillow/Google, then county records** (DISCO-746 and DISCO-756 descriptions, Suph, 8/17-8/21). ReportAll USA is LandGlide's backing data (Rob's 8/6 forward of the ReportAll thread).
- LaNae's framing (8/15, "Re: [LHRH] Bucees + ST Weekly Update 2026-08-14"): "we modify property attributes and apply exclusions (eg roof surfacing exclusion) in ~50% of those approvals. It's not a straight through approval."
- Nothing is learned from the corrections: the value overwrites, the source is thrown away, next month's quotes are pre-filled from the same feed (PRD BUC-5223 Problem statement, 8/24).
- Human attribute lookup only covers the 28% data job and does not prevent NOCs (Suph to Julie, DM 8/26). Prior art: the Nov-2025 AIT Pre-Bind Sunset readout (Curry): 25 VAs at $44k/mo doing full desk review, $254-1,692 per prevented NOC, killed (memory alert-north-star-sayback 8/26).
- Christine 8/16: the premium finding "points fingers again at our pre-fill quality" and belongs in "what needs to be true to remove UW alerts."

### 2c. Premium

- UW six-field corrections move premium **UP 3.4x as often as down** (median +$148 when it moves); **about +$699k net over the Jan-Jul window** on the DAMR cohort (MB 138284-138286, 8/7; memory damr-premium-join). Per field when UW moves it: property_type up 90% of the time, median +$376 (+$578 where the agent's edit had cut premium); construction +$108; roof_type +$86; sqft +$50; year_built +$60.
- Three premium figures are in circulation (see Section 5 #2): **~$26K/mo bound-only (~$310K/yr)** (What Must Be True 8/18; Two Houses; Dwelling Age Plan); **~$1.4M annualized / $67 per reviewed quote** all-approved (Suph to Christine 8/16); **"+$800K of GWP per year"** (Suph to Will Henry, DM 8/25: "Pre fill doesn't solve NOCs but those prefill modifications are about +$800K of GWP per year"). No file reconciles the $800K.
- Unreviewed old homes forgo **$3,034 per 100 bound** of pre-bind correction; ~12% recovered post-bind, **nothing at renewal** (median renewal change +3.2% just-under vs +3.5% DAMR vs +3.4% clean; underpricing persists into term 2). The $702k census figure is 78% never-bound, do not quote it as revenue (memory damr-postbind-uarnoe, 8/16).
- Roof type is premium-bearing: Hull Ave, slate to shingle plus sqft 1,748 to 2,040 moved premium $1,136 to $1,194 (+$58, ~5%).
- The "accurate does not equal bigger" caveat: premium nets up because classification and age fixes dominate, not because every correction is upward (Bake-off 8/17).
- Where the error is caught at inspection instead of quote: policy reformation back to inception with the premium difference back-charged to the insured, "a bad customer moment, a compliance exposure, and rework" (DISCO-746 description).

### 2d. Customer and agent experience

- Lost-looking CA deals: Kouvaris (SF four-plex, 8/17) and Ehrmann (LA, 9/1), both routed through ASM Marc Brune; Brune 8/18: agents "place business elsewhere"; 9/1: "seems to be costing us some biz."
- UW's stated workaround for CA agents: photos showing finishes above "Above Average" earn consideration for higher coverage (Kouvaris 8/17); agents can raise dwelling coverage 20% (Ehrmann 9/1).
- Datha (COO) found the basement gap herself in weekend testing (8/31); the reply has been drafted, fact-checked, and threaded (Suph 9/1 21:11 UTC on the LHRH thread); the +24% level question is deliberately kept out of it.
- New model exposure: in staging, property details show a basement "attributed to v360" while Steadily RCE silently ignores v360-sourced values, so "the customer won't know that it is not pricing the basement, and won't know to manually add it" (Curry 8/31, "an issue to resolve before shipping"). Five off-canvas fields are never sent to velma at all today (foundation shape, property slope, ceiling height, interior wall material, finished basement %), so "modifying them will never change the replacement cost estimate ... feel broken to users" (Joseph 9/1).
- Post-bind catch: coverage removed within 90 days of purchase with <$10 premium relief hits **6.79% of unreviewed** old-home policies vs **3.28% reviewed** (memory damr-postbind-uarnoe). Mostly roof exclusions, not data.
- LaNae 8/24 (RCE explainer thread): CX has "more arguments with lenders on this topic than we do with customers"; lenders want coverage to match the loan; most states bar lenders from requiring more than RCE, which is why they demand RCE documentation. Brent 8/24: most agents already understand market value vs replacement cost; an explainer belongs in SSB, not NAUI. Mitchell 8/25: first-time landlords call RCE a "black box" (DISCO-759, Parking lot).

### 2e. Renewals and post-bind: do we knock for pre-fill fields? No.

- Item-level UAR reasons on the unreviewed 91-100 twin: data-discrepancy reasons (residence type, year built, square footage) = **4 of 165 cancellation reason occurrences (2.4%)**, **zero for square footage**, all Tier 1 (fix at renewal). "We do not send NOCs for the six pre-filled data fields." ([README](https://github.com/suph-steadily/zero-alerts/blob/main/README.md) §6b-ii, pulled 8/26; memory alert-north-star-sayback)
- Inspection data-mismatch join (8/25): only **6 of 192** DAMR NOCs carried any mismatch flag, all cancelled for Liability/Condition/Ineligible (0 Misrep, 0 Pricing Change); mismatch-flagged policies cancel LESS (0.9% vs 5.2%). "Pre-fill errors surface premium-bearing (NOE lane), never as NOCs." (memory damr-postbind-uarnoe, 8/25 addendum)
- Post-bind attribute corrections are small everywhere: 0.7-1.3 per 100 bound, property_type-skewed, +$300 average (memory damr-postbind-uarnoe).
- Misrepresentation/Pricing NOC gap between reviewed and unreviewed: +0.20 per 100 (0.05 vs 0.25) ([README](https://github.com/suph-steadily/zero-alerts/blob/main/README.md) §6b).
- Suph's summary to Will 8/25 and standup 8/26: "pre-fill moves premium accuracy, not NOCs, so it may not be the right lever" for killing the alert.

### 2f. New-build roof score (BUC-5239)

- Cape scores new builds from mid-construction or pre-demolition imagery: **38% of year-built-2023+ policies have no roof score (vs 5% older)** and miss the ~10% new-roof discount; close rate measurably higher with it (memory buc-5239-cape-newbuild-override; ticket filed by Hartwig 2026-03, revived 8/26).
- The pre-fill angle (Suph 8/26 in the Hartwig/Greco/Jenna/Curry group DM): most "agent changed year built" events are agents replacing the **1980 fallback** (~960/mo, inspections agree 95%); the risky lane is a real pre-2015 year typed over to new (5.8%, ~218/mo, inspections disagree 40%), hiding behind very_poor Cape scores (22.5%) not blank ones (2.4%). Hartwig 8/27: "Does this mean 3/4 of very_poor might actually be very_good?" (open).

## Section 5. Contradictions and cautions

1. **Accurate does not equal bigger.** UW shrinks sqft on 58% of its sqft fixes (median 357 sq ft / 17.5%) and makes year built older on 71% of its fixes. Better pre-fill lowers RCE on those quotes. Premium still nets up 3.4:1 because classification (property_type) and age fixes dominate. The "pre-fill makes RCE low" story is true for missing features (kitchens, bedrooms, bathrooms, garage, basement, finishes) and false for square footage. (Bake-off 8/17.)
2. **Three premium figures, one loose one.** ~$26K/mo bound-only (~$310K/yr) is the artifact-safe number; ~$699k over 6 months / ~$1.4M annualized / $67 per reviewed quote is all-approved and 78% of the underlying approvals never bind; **"+$800K GWP/yr" (Suph to Will 8/25) has no written reconciliation** in any file read. Memory alert-north-star-sayback flags $1.4M as not reconciling with $310K. Pick one and say which denominator.
3. **28% vs "a third" vs "half."** 28.2% = share of DAMR approvals with a data fix (measured). "~1/3 UW updates our data even when present" = 9/1 kickoff, corrected in-meeting from "half," rollout context, not the same cohort. LaNae's "~50% of approvals" includes roof exclusions and other actions.
4. **Datha's case was re-diagnosed twice.** 8/31 Suph: neither source had the basement. 9/1 Rob: Smarty raw had basement_sqft 1804 (flows to the model) but the dwelling-assumptions prefill said Concrete Slab. 9/1 kickoff: "this wasn't even our model," her quote was valued by 360Value. The sent email says "neither Verisk nor Smarty returned basement data," while the fact-check doc argues for "pre-filled" not "returned." Do not restate the 8/31 version.
5. **Basement detection timeline moved.** Curry 8/10 (BUC-5006 comments): Pluribus MLS-image detection ~3-4 weeks out. Curry 8/31: "no plan to ship that anytime soon." No Jira ticket tracks it. (book business-logic.md)
6. **Sqft correction rate 8.4% vs 7.7%.** Bake-off (8/17) says sqft corrected on 8.4% of 10,407 reviewed quotes (ref to terminal version); flip validation (8/7) says 7.7% (802) of 10,428 approvals (ref to approval). Year built 2.5% vs 2.6% likewise. Different denominators and version endpoints; not a contradiction, but quote one consistently.
7. **DISCO-756 description has a mangled number.** It says "when agents modify prefill, UW is ~4x more likely to correct the value again" (artifacts say 1.4-5.7x, shorthand 2-6x) and "roof type ends up different from both the prefill and the agent's input 48% of the time." The artifacts say roof_type is 87% third value; 48% is the sqft back-to-pre-fill share (MB 138158). Treat the ticket text as stale.
8. **BUC-5097 status.** Jira shows Icebox (updated 9/1); the model change it describes (velma PR #130, Plinko v2.4.2) merged 8/21 and is the serving default. Jira status is stale, not the work.
9. **Model capability vs plumbing.** Curry's disposition doc says Villa takes finished_basement_percent, slope, interior wall finish. Joseph 9/1: the off-canvas does not send foundation shape, property slope, ceiling height, interior wall material, or finished basement % to velma, so editing them never moves the estimate. Both true: the model can price them, the request does not carry them yet.
10. **Steadily RCE vs 360Value level, unexplained.** $785,327 vs $635,062 (+24%) on identical basement-less inputs; Suph asked Curry 8/31 whether the recency-weighted retrain explains it; no answer as of 9/1. Held out of the Datha email. Relevant to Brent's "will it follow v360" question: the new model does not track 360Value's level.
11. **Not every low CA RCE is a pre-fill problem.** The SF case is proven inputs (same engine, same grade). The Goosehead $241/sq ft case has no inputs pulled; a cost-table or regional-cost story is equally possible until Marc's comps arrive. Marc's own 8/18 read ("30-50% lower on overhead/profit, framing, floor coverings") turned out to be downstream of inputs, not a different price book.
12. **Pre-fill does not cause NOCs.** 2.4% of twin cancellation reason occurrences are data discrepancies (zero sqft); 6 of 192 DAMR NOCs had any mismatch flag. Pre-fill errors show up as premium (NOE lane) and RCE, not cancellations. This is why the pre-fill workstream was ranked last in Will's alert-work order and why the PRD is "held."
13. **"Make the agent type it" is not a free fix.** Agent-entered values are corrected 2-6x more often, but the sample is selection-biased (agents edit visibly broken quotes) and some of it is definitional (living area vs county total, basement in or out). The pre-fill flip (BUC-4962, Cameron's hypothesis) was effectively disproven 8/7 (Cameron: "sad that you seem to have already disproven my hypothesis"; Christine: "damn... we had such a good plan!"). Gaming exists but is ~2%.
14. **What the stamp means.** Per-field SMARTY just means 360Value echoed the Smarty seed; VALUE_360 is the fall-through; the stamp is inferred at read time, not recorded at fetch. Do not read the stamps as a vendor accuracy scoreboard.
15. **In the Steadily RCE world, 360Value-sourced pre-fill disappears.** Curry's "Lost" column (bedrooms 17.3%, exterior wall 12.7%, stories 9.0%, bathrooms 8.4%, basement 8.1%) is what Smarty cannot replace. Curry's position: already priced into the model metrics; the 9/1 kickoff position: leave blanks blank, pricing handles them, launch stays consistent with validation. Datha's case shows how a blank reads to a human.
16. **"Working around for years."** The Datha email says "the same pre-fill data gap we have been working around for years"; the fact-check cites "three years of attempts" from the 9/1 kickoff (Granola). Stated, not verified here.
17. **UW's "wrong more often than not" vs the measured 3-8%.** DISCO-746 records UW's 8/6 perception; the measured UW edit rate per field on approvals is 3-8%, 28.2% on any field. Perception and measurement differ by field and denominator; DISCO-746 exists precisely because "there is no measured error rate today."
18. **Marc's 8/18 premise was wrong.** "My understanding is that we are basing our tool on the current version of 360Value" (about the coming RCE update). Steadily RCE is a separate in-house model; the SF quote itself was on 360Value.
19. **The Curry/Rob RCE explainer is a different problem.** "Why the RCE we sometimes quote is so much lower than what the home is worth" (Curry 8/24) is market value vs rebuild cost; LaNae asked what problem it solves; Brent said agents already understand the distinction. It should not be conflated with the CA low-RCE complaints.
20. **Owner of the pre-fill impact measurement.** Kickoff notes say Rob; Curry did BUC-5271 and the sheet; Suph's sent email names David. Confirm before the 9/10 call.
