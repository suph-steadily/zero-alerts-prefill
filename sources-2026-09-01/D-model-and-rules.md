Research memo for Suph, 2026-09-01. Read-only. Sources: ralphie/frizzle/arnold-1 local clones, velma + ryerson via `gh api` (read-only), Jira, Confluence, Google Drive, Slack, and the originating Gmail thread. Every claim below is cited; inferences are labeled.

## Plain-language answers (paste-ready)

- **The number the agent is seeing is 360Value's number, and our own model is built to match it.** The Steadily model (Plinko, in velma) is trained to reproduce what 360Value has been putting on our quotes. Its own version notes call it a "v360 emulator" and its validation target is agreement with 360Value (median difference -0.12%, 88% of homes within 20%). In our own data, 360Value's median for Los Angeles County homes of 2,500 to 3,000 sq ft is about $255/sq ft; the agent's $241 is right in that range. Switching the model, by itself, will not move LA numbers.

- **But we own every lever, and 360Value gave us none.** The model is a Craftsman cost-book calculation (with a location factor by area, plus state and county inputs) corrected by machine learning, then adjusted by rating factors we set in versioned config files. State-level calibration overrides already exist for NV and AZ. A "California adjustment" is technically a config and retraining decision Steadily controls, not something we ask Verisk for. The open question is not "can we," it is "should we, by how much, and who signs off" (UW, actuarial, and the team that built the model). That decision has not been made or asked.

- **Our own data science already flagged California once.** David Curry's first production read (Dec 12, 2025) listed "California Geographic Divergence" as a top error source and wrote "I suspect Plinko is more correct here." In the Bay Area, Plinko said ~$400/sq ft where 360Value said ~$246; in SoCal the two were close (~$280 vs ~$271). Later versions were deliberately tuned closer to 360Value (county floors that pushed us above 360Value in expensive markets were turned off). So the model can disagree with 360Value in CA; today it is set not to.

- **The "20%" is real and it is a code rule, not a UW judgment call.** Coverage A on an agent (inde) quote must sit between 100% and 120% of the RCE (ralphie + ryerson, in place since June 2023). A wider band (90% to 150%, DP3 only) shipped in July 2026 but only for our direct self-serve funnel, under a LaunchDarkly flag; independent agents do not get it. UW can lift the ceiling by entering an RCE override, which then allows up to 120% of the override, but there is no written policy for when UW does that, and nothing in the UW guides says an outside carrier's RCE, appraisal, or contractor bid is accepted. The written UW method is to fix the inputs (sq ft, quality, roof, basement) so the number moves, not to accept someone else's number.

- **Inputs matter as much as the model, and the ones that move the estimate most are the ones most often wrong.** UW's own 360Value guide names the "Big Three" as finished square footage, roof material, and quality grade, plus construction type, number of units, finished basement, and attached garage. In the Steadily model, quality grade is the largest single lever (Standard to Above Average about +14%, to Custom about +38%, to Premium about +79%); slope adds up to +12%, plaster walls +7%, cabinets up to +6%. A basic-quality label on a semi-custom LA home would by itself explain a big chunk of a "half of true cost" gap. Note that Custom/Premium triggers a UW alert asking for plans, permits, invoices, or appraisal.

- **California is not in any rollout phase yet.** Every plan says Arizona (and Nevada) first, direct then inde, hold at least a month, then "expand slower than Wilson did; expansion batches TBD." The CA replacement-cost regulation (10 CCR 2695.183) has already shaped which fields we added (kitchens, interior finishes, cabinets, slope), so CA compliance is being designed for, but no CA date exists.

## Q1. The model (Plinko, velma repo)

### What it is and what it is trained on

Recency-weighted training (exponential, half-life 6 months) over 2025-01 to 2026-06 to track the +4.7%/15mo drift of the target against the Craftsman baseline. ## Q4. Which inputs move the estimate most

360Value (UW's own written view, Confluence "360Value RCE Review Guide", TU 3042181301, Nov 2025):
- "The Big Three": Total Finished Square Feet, Roof Material, Quality Grade. High impact also: Address/ZIP ("drives localized cost data"), # of Units, Construction Type, Interior Finishes, Finished Basement (entered separately), Attached Garage. Moderate: Year Built, # Stories, # Bathrooms. Low: trim, fixtures, paint, wall height.
- Rules of thumb: "Missing 300 sqft can understate Cov A by $50,000-$75,000." "Finished basements often add $50K-$100K." "Misrating a 'Custom' home as 'Standard' can skew Cov A by $100K or more." "Roof material alone can shift valuation by $30K-$75K+."
- Measured 360Value toggles: BUC-5097 description: "Plaster to drywall took one staging quote from $262k to $245k" (-6.5%). Christine Luo, BUC-2803 2025-06-18 (RBT-3670): RCE fell 73% ($680K to $175K) from single family -> townhome, Standard -> Economy, sqft 4,898 -> 1,127, "mostly attributable to sq ft." Darren 2025-06-16: agents can push 360Value to 200% of the initial RCE via inputs.
- Pre-fill misses that would explain a low LA number (inference from the above): quality labeled Standard on an Above Average/Custom home; roof entered as shingle on a tile roof (tile is the SoCal norm); finished basement or living-over-garage omitted; unit count wrong; sqft from a stale record. Book: carlos hard defaults sqft 2300 / year built 1980 when 360Value errors; Curry Dec-2025 doc: "Default Sqft Fallback (2300 sqft) (41%) ... ~35% inflated errors."

Steadily model (Google Doc "Villa - BUC-5057 Dwelling Assumption Fields - Disposition (2026-08-17)", Drive `1GBH85MmpgSIg3dyqtDZMOkynS4Fs8kFB`, linked from BUC-5097; and `docs/plinko.md`):
- Home quality grade: "the largest lever we have." vs Standard: Economy -10.2%, Above Average +13.7%, Custom +37.6%, Premium +79.2% (range +62.7% to +92.9%). `docs/plinko.md`: "Economy to Premium spans +42.6% to +135.9% of the final number."
- Property slope: moderate +6%, steep +12% (Verisk published). Interior wall material plaster +7%. Cabinets premium +6%, countertops marble +3% (pair capped +8%). Interior finish luxury +5% / minimum -5%. Kitchens +5% each (max 3, single-unit only). Floor covering -3% to +2%. Exterior wall: stucco +5.4%, brick/masonry +6.9%, wood +8.9%, stone +9.0%. Finished basement % up to +3.8%. Garage style: basement -3.5%, carport -2.4% to -6%.
- Structure: sqft is linear; stories +4% per story above the first; masonry x1.08-1.10; multi-family x1.30 + 0.14 per unit over 2; roof type 0.98x to 1.20x (tile 1.08-1.12, slate 1.15); condo 0.30x; manufactured 0.45x; geographic modifier applied to the whole base.
- Dollar table on a $522,567 median home: slope none->steep +$62,708; drywall->plaster +$36,580; cabinets standard->premium +$31,354; interior standard->luxury +$26,128; kitchens 1->2 +$26,128.
- Caveat (docs/plinko.md): all 11 post-ML rating fields "arrive on 0.0% of requests" from carlos today (agent edits not yet wired for several; see book 8/31 issues), so today only quality grade, sqft, roof, construction, foundation/basement, garage and the location features actually move the Steadily number.
