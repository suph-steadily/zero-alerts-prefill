# Pre-fill's second customer: the replacement cost estimate

**Date:** 2026-09-02 · **Owner:** Suph · **Status:** evidence folded in from the 9/1 research for Brent's CA thread and from the Goodbye 360Value rollout work (8/31 to 9/1). Read-only research. Nothing here changed a system.

**Why this file exists.** On 8/26 the pre-fill workstream (BUC-5223, DISCO-746, DISCO-756) was ranked last in the alert work because pre-fill errors do not cause NOCs (README §6b-ii: 2.4% of cancellation reasons, zero for square footage). Between 8/17 and 9/1 the same pre-fill surfaced as two other things: the cause of "our RCE is too low" complaints that cost binds directly, and the central risk of the Steadily RCE rollout. This memo is the project's record of that. README §5b is the short version. The raw research memos (fact sheet, history, model and rules) are in [sources-2026-09-01/](sources-2026-09-01/).

Plain words: RCE = replacement cost estimate, the rebuild cost the dwelling limit (Coverage A) is set from. 360Value = Verisk's RCE product, on every quote today. Steadily RCE = our own model (project name Villa, model name Plinko, served from the velma repo), rolling out direct and Arizona first (BUC-5155). Pre-fill = the property data we buy and fill in before anyone types.

---

## 1. The claim

The six fields underwriters fix on dwelling-age reviews, plus bedrooms, bathrooms, kitchens, garage, basement and finishes, feed two machines: the underwriting review and the RCE. When the pre-fill leaves things out, the RCE comes out low, the agent reads it as underinsuring, and the business goes elsewhere. That is a bind cost the alert work never counted. The Steadily RCE model makes it bigger in two ways: it only prices what it is given, and it never uses values that came from 360Value, so whatever Smarty cannot supply goes blank when 360Value goes away. And the model learned its levels from 360Value's answers on our own pre-filled quotes, so the pre-fill's gaps are plausibly baked into what it learned to match (inference, to confirm with David).

Two things to keep apart, always: the **model** (the engine) and the **inputs** (the pre-fill). The SF four-plex is proven inputs. The Calabasas case reads as model level. Say "inputs" only where the inputs were pulled.

Julie's framing for the Datha reply is the one to reuse: this is the biggest known gap for computing an RCE, and it is not limited to Steadily RCE.

## 2. How each pre-fill error moves the RCE

Sizes are Steadily RCE (Plinko v2.4.2) modifiers from David Curry's "Villa - BUC-5057 Dwelling Assumption Fields - Disposition (2026-08-17)" gdoc and velma `docs/plinko.md`. 360Value's exact factors are not documented in our files; the SF case shows the same direction empirically.

| Field | When the pre-fill is wrong or missing, the RCE goes | Size of the lever | Where the value comes from today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchens | DOWN | 2 = +5%, 3 = +10%, 4+ = +15% (single-unit homes; multi-family uses the unit multiplier) | Not in Smarty. Default 1. The SF four-plex's biggest miss (2 pre-filled vs 4) |
| Bedrooms, bathrooms | DOWN | Consumed by the model (Smarty-enriched; user-entered values via velma PR #131) | Smarty misses one or the other ~35% of the time (Joseph, 8/31, db 268); partial baths populated 13% |
| Basement | DOWN | Finished basement % adds up to +3.8% on top of the basement itself; UW's 360Value guide: "finished basements often add $50K-$100K" | Smarty `basement_sqft` when present, passed straight to the model (Rob 9/1). No detection otherwise: "no plan to ship that anytime soon" (Curry 8/31) |
| Garage | DOWN | Attached or built-in = full enclosed-garage rate; basement drive-under -3.5%; carport -2.4% to -6% | Smarty garage, 100% filled (Curry's table) |
| Home quality grade | DOWN when a Custom home is labeled Standard. The largest lever in the model | vs Standard: Economy -10.2%, Above Average +13.7%, Custom +37.6%, Premium +79.2% | ZIP-based inference when missing. Custom and Premium require proof for UW |
| Finishes, slope, interior walls | DOWN when the truth is nicer than the default | Plaster +7%, steep slope +12%, luxury interior +5%, premium cabinets +6% | Five off-canvas fields (foundation shape, slope, ceiling height, interior wall material, finished basement %) never reach the model today (Joseph 9/1) |
| Exterior wall, stories | Depends on the truth | Stucco +5.4%, brick +6.9%, wood +8.9%, stone +9.0%; +4% per story above the first | Lost on 12.7% and 9.0% of quotes when 360Value goes away (Curry) |
| **Square footage** | **UP on the quotes UW fixes.** The feed skews BIG | Linear | UW shrinks sqft on 58% of its sqft fixes, median 357 sq ft / 17.5%. Hard default 2,300 when 360Value errors |
| Year built | Not documented for the RCE; premium goes UP when fixed | | Feed skews NEW: 71% of UW fixes make the home older, median 10 years. Hard default 1980 |

The sentence to keep straight: "pre-fill makes the RCE low" is true for missing features (kitchens, bedrooms, bathrooms, garage, basement, finishes, quality) and false for square footage.

## 5. The model side

What is verified (velma repo at HEAD via the GitHub API, read 9/1; Drive docs named inline):

- **What it is.** Plinko v2.4.2, `MODEL_VERSIONS.yaml`: "CovA-target v360 emulator ... Recency-weighted training (exponential, half-life 6 months) over 2025-01 to 2026-06 ... Validated on the July 2026 temporal holdout vs v360 RCE: median bias -0.12%, within-20% 88.34%, under-by-20% 5.31%." `docs/rate_factor_basis.md` opens: "Villa's job is to reproduce V360/Verisk as closely as our data allows." Training rows 590,599; validation 147,643; 274 features; trained 2026-08-18.
- **What it is trained on.** Target = 360Value's RCE on our own quotes and issued policies (`residual = v360_rce - craftsman_rce`). The training filter prefers records where 360Value used system defaults, that is, our pre-fill, over records an agent had changed ("clean ground truth where V360 used pushpin/system defaults"). Not trained on claims or contractor estimates; the claims team uses Xactimate fresh at claim time (Darren, BUC-2803).
- **So the pre-fill is in the target (inference).** If the pre-fill systematically under-describes homes (missing kitchens, rooms, basements), 360Value's answer on those homes was low for that reason, and the model learned to reproduce that level. David's own validation note points the same way (7/30 email "Steadily RCE vs 360Value: what does Datha's bar look like?" to Datha, Jeff Greco and Bryan Hartwig, cc Christine/Julie/Suph/Rob; gdoc 13MNBmiGrcxs3ZBFw7LndxZ9Z0WuXyoiy; companion doc "Explaining Why Steadily RCE Under-Prices by >20% vs V360", Plinko v2.3.2, n=129,752): agreement within 20% on 91.9% of homes, no overall bias; the under-insurance risk is the 5% priced more than 20% below 360Value; cause 1 = missed basements, 31% of the under-priced homes ("our basement $/sqft rates run about half of v360's"); cause 2 = expensive-for-market homes "hidden to us behind a Standard quality label", more than half the under-priced tail; and "the model compresses toward the middle exactly where it lacks per-home facts to justify an unusual value." Both causes are pre-fill shaped. Better pre-fill is better training data. Confirm the training-target mechanism with David before quoting it as fact.
- **David's success bar and his data ask.** Bar for the AZ rollout (8/11): median error under 10%, bias under 2%, large errors (>20%) under 10% of predictions; green light AZ only, 30-day review. 8/11 he asked Suph about keeping some Verisk data services (basement, quality) while winding down 360Value, "this would solve both basement and quality issues." Tim McCullagh 8/29: Verisk is "not budging" on shifting committed 360Value spend. So the vendor path for basement and quality data is unresolved.
- **Premium check in flight.** Curry weekly 8/21: off-balancing AZ with Steadily RCE in place of 360Value to measure the expected premium delta (Jeff Greco's ask); "if we see drastic premium shifts in the aggregate, we may pause rollout."
- **Levers Steadily owns, 360Value never exposed.** Craftsman area factors (LA ZIP3 900-916 +8 to +10%, SF +26%), quality tiers, 11 post-ML rating curves, `calibration.json` with state overrides (NV and AZ exist, no CA), global cost bounds $125 to $800/sqft, training window and target. A California adjustment is a config or retrain decision for UW, actuarial and David's team. Nobody has asked for it as of 9/2.
- **360Value's own LA level in our book.** `v360_psf_lookup.json` (850K rows): CA state median $266/sqft; Los Angeles County $255/sqft at 2,500 to 3,000 sqft ($344 at 500-800 sqft down to $221 at 6,000-8,000). The Calabasas $241 is about 5% under that. Curry's 2025-12-12 production read flagged "California Geographic Divergence ... I suspect Plinko is more correct here" (Bay Area 360Value $246 vs Plinko $400/sqft; SoCal $271 vs $280); later versions were tuned back toward 360Value and county floors were removed.
- **Today only some fields move the Steadily number.** All 11 post-ML rating fields "arrive on 0.0% of requests" from carlos (`docs/plinko.md`), so quality grade, sqft, roof, construction, foundation/basement, garage and the location features are what actually move it until the plumbing lands.
- **The 20% rule is code, not judgment.** Coverage A must sit at 1.0 to 1.2x the RCE for every inde quote since June 2023 (ralphie `dwelling.py:61`; ryerson `DEFAULT_RCE_RANGE`). The 0.9 to 1.5x band (GLD-1538, July 2026) is direct self-serve DP3 only. UW can lift the ceiling with a numeric RCE override. No written UW policy on accepting another carrier's RCE, appraisal or bid; the written method is to fix the inputs. The coupling cuts both ways: TECHSUP-4387 (8/28), a roof edit re-pulled 360Value, the RCE fell 33% ($422k to $281k) and Coverage A was silently clamped to 1.2x ($493k to $338k) with the decrease auto-approved; the insured threatened a DOI complaint. A band-widening proposal exists (Brian Kim, "Cov A and RCE", 7/29 to 7/30: 90 to 120% in agreed-value states, 90 to 150% elsewhere, require a reason when editing RCE inputs, a Coverage A slider; Cosmo to pick up in the six-week roadmap). It would answer the Calabasas agent's 20% complaint structurally; not built for inde agents as of 9/2.
- **CA is in no rollout phase.** Every plan: AZ (and NV), direct then inde, hold a month, "expansion batches TBD."

## 7. Cautions

1. **Accurate does not equal bigger.** Square footage skews big and year built skews new. Better pre-fill lowers the RCE on those quotes. Premium still nets up 3.4:1 because classification and age fixes dominate.
2. **Three premium figures.** ~$26K/mo bound-only (~$310K/yr) is the safe one. ~$1.4M annualized counts approvals that never bound (78%). "+$800K GWP/yr" (Suph to Will 8/25) has no written reconciliation. Pick one, name the denominator.
3. **Datha's case was re-diagnosed twice.** 8/31: neither source had the basement. 9/1: Smarty raw had it, the dwelling-assumptions pre-fill said slab. Kickoff: her quote was still valued by 360Value. Do not restate the 8/31 version.
4. **Not every low CA RCE is pre-fill.** SF four-plex = inputs, proven. Calabasas = reads model level, quote never pulled. Marc's 8/18 guess ("30-50% lower on overhead, framing, floor coverings") turned out to be downstream of inputs, not a different price book.
5. **Model capability vs plumbing.** The model can price finished basement %, slope and wall finish; the request does not carry them yet. Both true.
6. **28% vs "a third" vs "half."** 28.2% = share of dwelling-age approvals with a data fix (measured). "~1/3 UW updates our data even when present" = 9/1 kickoff, rollout context, different cohort. LaNae's "~50% of approvals" includes roof exclusions.
7. **The "learned from our pre-fill" claim is an inference** from how the model is trained. Verified pieces: the target, the training filter, the under-pricing-tail analysis. Not verified: that the bias is material at the level of a CA home. Ask David.
8. **The stamps are not a vendor scoreboard.** Per-field SMARTY means 360Value echoed the Smarty seed; VALUE_360 is the fall-through; the stamp is inferred at read time.

1. **Pre-fill gets a second value line.** The alert case for pre-fill was avoided UW touches plus ~$310K/yr of premium behind the wall. Add: business lost to low RCEs. Unsized today.
